The consent page now runs the live identity chaining exchange for Speakeasy-issued sign-ins, as it already does for external identity providers. A service that accepts the user shows "Connected through your identity provider" before approval, and one that refuses them shows the refusal and blocks "Give access" instead of failing on the first tool call. An ambiguous refusal of a signed assertion right after a signing key is published reads as neutral, since the service may not have fetched the key yet; configuration, client and scope refusals still block. The credential obtained on the consent page belongs to that sign-in, and any grant backed by the same sign-in reuses it on first use instead of signing a second assertion. Approving before the check finishes keeps the earlier behavior: the first tool call runs the exchange. Revoking the sign-in, rotating the issuer key or changing the binding before approval leaves the credential unused, denying consent erases the credentials stored at that moment, and a consent check never replaces another grant's or another sign-in's stored credential. Reloading the consent page does not redeem again.
