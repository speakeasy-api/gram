Record the SSO sign-in behind each MCP grant and check, before Speakeasy vouches for a user to another service, that the grant still traces to a current SSO sign-in: not revoked, within the method's maximum age of when the user actually authenticated at WorkOS, made with the email WorkOS verified at that sign-in (a later profile email change waits for a fresh sign-in), still a member, not deprovisioned, on one of the organization's verified domains. Sign-ins are recorded only in the organization the user signed in to, and a grant is never minted from a sign-in an administrator, sign-out everywhere or deprovisioning revoked, including one still being recorded when the revocation landed. `auth.logoutEverywhere` ends every dashboard and identity provider session and revokes the user's sign-ins and the grants made from them; `organizations.revokeMemberSignIns` revokes a member's sign-ins and grants in the active organization only, and leaves their dashboard sessions alone. Revocations reach the token cache and WorkOS through a durable queue retried every five minutes. Making new links, evidence lookups, deprovisioning revocation and both revocation endpoints are behind the `sso-sign-in-enforcement` flag, evaluated per organization; with it off nothing new is linked; links already made keep being honoured. Once a user has signed out everywhere, dashboard sessions issued before then are rejected whatever the flag says. Existing grants keep working and are simply not linked.

Which sign-ins can back an assertion now depends on the method, each with its own maximum age: enterprise SSO (7 days); Google sign-in on a Google Workspace account that belongs to one of the organization's verified domains, or Microsoft sign-in on a work account in a tenant the organization pinned (24 hours); magic link (4 hours); and password sign-in, with or without a second factor (4 hours). Google and Microsoft evidence needs WorkOS to return OAuth tokens for those connections. Personal Google or Microsoft accounts never qualify. Every method is allowed by default, and an organization can limit them on its issuer.
