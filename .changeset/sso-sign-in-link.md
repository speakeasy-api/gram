Record the SSO sign-in behind each MCP grant and check, before Speakeasy vouches for a user to another service, that the grant still traces to a current SSO sign-in: not revoked, within the method's maximum age of when the user actually authenticated at WorkOS, made with the email WorkOS verified at that sign-in (a later profile email change waits for a fresh sign-in), still a member, not deprovisioned, on one of the organization's verified domains. Sign-ins are recorded only in the organization the user signed in to, and a grant is never minted from a sign-in an administrator, sign-out everywhere or deprovisioning revoked, including one still being recorded when the revocation landed. `auth.logoutEverywhere` ends every dashboard and identity provider session and revokes the user's sign-ins and the grants made from them; `organizations.revokeMemberSignIns` does the same for a member of the active organization. Revocations reach the token cache and WorkOS through a durable queue retried hourly. Linking, evidence lookups, deprovisioning revocation and both revocation endpoints are behind the `sso-sign-in-enforcement` flag, evaluated per organization; with it off nothing changes. Once a user has signed out everywhere, dashboard sessions issued before then are rejected whatever the flag says. Existing grants keep working and are simply not linked.

Which sign-ins can back an assertion now depends on the method, each with its own maximum age: enterprise SSO (7 days); Google sign-in on a Google Workspace account that belongs to one of the organization's verified domains, or Microsoft sign-in on a work account in a tenant the organization pinned (24 hours); magic link (4 hours); and password sign-in with an enrolled TOTP factor, only where `GRAM_WORKOS_MFA_ENFORCED` asserts WorkOS requires MFA (4 hours). Google and Microsoft evidence needs WorkOS to return OAuth tokens for those connections. Password sign-ins without a second factor and personal Google or Microsoft accounts never qualify. Every method is allowed by default, and an organization can limit them on its issuer.
